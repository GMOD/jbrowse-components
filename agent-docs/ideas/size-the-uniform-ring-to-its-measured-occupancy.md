`MAX_UNIFORM_SLOTS` is 2048 and `webgpuHal.ts` allocates all of them at
construction, as a GPU buffer and as a `Uint8Array` staging copy, sized by the
largest uniform struct any registered pass declares. For alignments that is 640
bytes, which rounds to a 768-byte slot, so **1.57 MB of GPU buffer and 1.57 MB
of JS heap per alignments display** — and a frame of it uses
4<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.6-alignments-tracks-dpr-2.peakSlots--> slots, or
0.20%<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.6-alignments-tracks-dpr-2.slotShare--> of what
was allocated. The table above is
`products/jbrowse-web/browser-tests/probe-uniform-ring.ts`, which wraps the
page's own `createBuffer`, `createTexture` and `queue.writeBuffer`.

## The palette pack is declined

The proposal this file replaces was to pack `readCategoryColor` from
`float4[23]` (368 bytes) to `uint4[6]` (96) — the one field big enough to take
the struct under 512 bytes and so the ring slot from 768 to 512. **Both halves
of it check out.** slangc compiles the hand-indexed vector form on both
backends: the segfault `colorPack.slang` records is the wrapper struct a scalar
`uint[N]` element gets, and a `uint4` element has none. `pnpm gen:shaders` emits
`UNIFORMS_SIZE_BYTES = 368` with its layout, shared-struct and device-px
cross-checks green (run 2026-09-07 and reverted). The read is in the vertex
stage, 9 vertices per read, so the unpack is four ALU ops where there were none.

It is declined on what it buys.
2.10MB<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.2-alignments-tracks-dpr-1.packSaves--> across
two tracks,
6.29MB<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.6-alignments-tracks-dpr-2.packSaves--> across
six — half GPU, half heap — on a session whose displays are already holding
223.31MB<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.6-alignments-tracks-dpr-2.msaa--> of MSAA
colour attachment that nothing counts
([ARCHITECTURAL_LIMITS.md](../reference/ARCHITECTURAL_LIMITS.md) §"The MSAA
target is the largest per-display allocation"). The ring is
4.0%<!--m:uniform-ring-occupancy.6-alignments-tracks-dpr-2.ringShare--> of that
session's GPU bytes; the pack takes it to 2.7%. In exchange the CPU side stops
using the generated `setUniformReadCategoryColor` — whose whole job is that a
caller cannot forget a lane — for `[slot >> 2] + (slot & 3)` word arithmetic
spelled the same way in two places.

The abandoned `worktree-arc-uniform-split` branch implemented it, and its commit
message is where the 4 MiB → 2 MiB figure comes from. That was written before
`8fc803d14d` banked the arc band's 160 bytes on main, so it is one halving out
of date: from 640 the pack is 3 MB → 2 MB per track.

## Where the megabytes actually are

The pack recovers a third of the ring. Sizing the ring to what a frame uses
recovers essentially all of it — three times as much, with no shader change at
all — but it is a bigger change than a smaller constant, and two things have to
come first:

- **The cap has to stay a cap.** Overrunning it does not throw: the write is
  dropped and its draws render against another batch's uniforms, which is wrong
  pixels with no error. So this is a growth path — recreate the buffer, the
  shared `uniformOnlyBindGroup` and every entry in `passBindGroups` — rather
  than a lower number.
- **The worst case is not measured.** Alignments writes once per stacked section
  per block, plus one per section with an arc band, so a grouped view multiplies
  by up to `MAX_GROUPS` (40) and a multi-region view by the block count again.
  The 4 above is two groups and two blocks. Bound that shape before picking a
  starting size.

Same measure-first note as
[cut-webgl2-contexts-per-display](cut-webgl2-contexts-per-display.md), and it
applies to the whole axis: nothing has yet shown that GPU bytes are what a real
session is short of, and if they are, the attachment is two orders of magnitude
ahead of the ring in the queue.
